Three Redesign Directions

Each approach solves all three problems — but with a different philosophy. Pick the one that feels right.

A

Guided Setup Hub

A dedicated onboarding wizard takes over after signup. Users work through 4 setup steps before reaching the dashboard. Marketing page focuses on the "save money" story with a single strong CTA.

Landing: Big hero + 1 CTA
Post-signup: 4-step wizard
Nav: 5 tabs, clean + simple
Dashboard: Progress-first

Best for: Getting new users set up fast and reducing confusion on first use. Works like Duolingo or Headspace onboarding.

B

Command Center

No separate wizard — instead, the dashboard itself becomes the guide. A persistent "setup progress" card lives at the top until you complete all 4 steps. Marketing page is a scrolling story: problem → solution → proof.

Landing: Scroll story w/ demos
Post-signup: Dashboard w/ checklist
Nav: Goal-based labels
Dashboard: Everything in one hub

Best for: Users who want to explore at their own pace. Feels like Notion or Linear — powerful but you guide yourself.

C

Problem-First Flow

Marketing page leads with pain ("You're leaking $240/month"). A quick quiz during signup personalizes the experience. Post-login shows only what's relevant to the user's goals — no overwhelming feature dump.

Landing: Pain-point hero
Signup: 3-question quiz
Nav: Personalized to goals
Dashboard: Goal-matched view

Best for: Higher signup conversion. Works like Rocket Money or Nerdwallet — lead with the problem they already feel.

All three approaches include:
Simplified navigation (max 5-6 items visible)
Step-by-step setup: income → subs → bills → goals
Mobile-optimized layout throughout
Clear "what to do next" prompts everywhere
Progress indicators so nothing feels lost
Single strong CTA on the landing page
